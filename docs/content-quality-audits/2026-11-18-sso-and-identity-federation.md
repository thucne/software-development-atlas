## Standards, source, examples and scoped correctness

1. **SSO and separated sessions:** Per [OpenID Connect Core](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0-18.html), each Relying Party (RP) validates the OP response intended for itself; reusing the OP's authentication context does not mean App A and App B share a cookie or resource authorization. The sequence is explicitly an illustrative summary rather than a byte-accurate HTTP trace: the browser participates in front-channel redirects while a confidential client may exchange a code on its backend.
2. **SAML trust:** [OASIS SAML 2.0 Technical Overview](https://docs.oasis-open.org/security/saml/Post2.0/sstc-saml-tech-overview-2.0.html) describes Assertion Consumer Services, bindings, signature validation and service-provider trust. An SP must account for issuer, audience, destination/recipient, time restrictions and replay as applicable; a mathematically valid signature from a wrong domain or intended audience does not authorize login. Identity mapping and downstream authorization remain application-owned.
3. **CAS one-time service tickets:** [Apereo CAS protocol specification](https://apereo.github.io/cas/development/protocol/CAS-Protocol-Specification.html) states a Service Ticket is bound to the service and **valid for only one validation attempt**, whether that attempt succeeds or fails. A CAS Ticket Granting Cookie represents a different central SSO state. Revised both languages to avoid presenting a Service Ticket as a reusable bearer credential or as valid for any application.
4. **Logout best effort:** [OIDC RP-Initiated Logout](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-rpinitiated-1_0-final.html) and OIDC front-/back-channel mechanisms depend on integration and delivery. Completing logout at App A or the OP does not atomically clear App B's local session, an offline RP, or every issued bearer token. SAML logout can face similar partial-notification limits. In the exercise, deleting App A's cookie does not invalidate App B.
5. **Brokering versus user federation:** Comparing the example against Keycloak's [identity brokering](https://www.keycloak.org/docs/latest/server_admin/#_identity_broker) and [user federation](https://www.keycloak.org/docs/latest/server_admin/#_user-storage-federation) descriptions: delegating authentication to another IdP is different from consulting a user directory/credential store such as LDAP. Neither removes API-side object authorization.
6. **Threat example and non-executable artifacts:** The silent re-login after App A's local logout is explicitly hypothetical; no observed incident, universal cookie guarantee or timing metric is claimed. Read and compared all four diagrams and protocol-table roles. No live IdP, SAML assertion parser, CAS server, browser federation or logout fault injection was run; the illustrations were checked statically against specifications instead.
